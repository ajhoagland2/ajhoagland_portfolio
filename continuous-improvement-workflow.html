A system-level Artemis return chute optimization project investigating production variability across equipment behavior, operator actions, material flow, and downstream recovery.

Senior Systems Case Study

ARTEMIS RETURN CHUTE OPTIMIZATION

Problem

The Artemis production system experienced performance fluctuations that were often attributed to equipment behavior alone. In practice, output was shaped by machine performance, operator interaction, material flow characteristics, and downstream recovery processes.

Constraints

Existing production could not be significantly interrupted. Multiple variables influenced performance at the same time, and available data often suggested equipment faults even when operator and process variables were contributing factors.

Solution

Conducted a system-level investigation of the return chute and associated workflow, analyzing interactions between equipment operation, operator actions, material flow, and production recovery methods. Developed process modifications and design improvements to reduce disruptions and improve material handling consistency.

Results

Improved understanding of production variability drivers, identified hidden interactions between operator behavior and equipment performance, reduced reliance on anecdotal troubleshooting, and established measurable inputs for evaluating system performance.

Value Created

Shifted troubleshooting from component-level assumptions to system-level analysis, reduced time spent pursuing incorrect root causes, improved visibility into throughput variation, and created a repeatable methodology for future manufacturing bottlenecks.

03 / Fixture Development

WAT GRID BRACKET DEVELOPMENT

Senior Systems Case Study

FROM OPERATOR-DEPENDENT SETUP TO REPEATABLE ASSEMBLY

Problem

Assembly grids required consistent positioning and orientation during production. Existing methods allowed unnecessary movement, increased operator dependency, and created opportunities for assembly variation. Debris trapped between the monitor surface and grid could also negatively affect assembly quality.

Constraints

The fixture had to integrate with existing WAT workstation layouts, avoid adding operator complexity, support repeatable positioning across multiple assemblies, and manage debris passively without creating additional maintenance burden.

Solution

Designed and developed a standardized fixture system that maintains fixed grid positioning, enforces assembly orientation, reduces operator decision-making, and allows debris and loose tile fragments to exit beneath the fixture.

Results

Standardized grid placement across operators, reduced opportunities for assembly misalignment, improved workstation consistency, and simplified training through built-in process guidance.

Value Created

Reduced assembly variation by physically constraining alignment conditions, eliminated common orientation mistakes through fixture-based controls, embedded process knowledge into hardware, and created a repeatable assembly platform for multiple workstations.
